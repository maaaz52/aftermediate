1. Executive Summary

aftermediate is a purpose-built, AI-assisted career-counseling platform for Pakistani intermediate-level students. Every year, hundreds of thousands of FSc, ICS, I.Com and A-Level students finish school with almost no reliable, individualised guidance about what to do next. Decisions are made on rumour, coaching-academy marketing, and parental pressure — with life-long financial consequences.

The product closes that gap with a single, honest, data-backed path:

  1. Collect the student's real position — stream, marks, entry-test plans, budget, parental pressure, skills — via a short, resumable 7-section onboarding quiz.
  2. Compute real merit numbers using the actual university aggregate formulas (NUST, FAST, PMDC/MDCAT, UET), so the student sees their true standing instead of an academy's marketing number.
  3. Counsel with an AI guide (Rahbar) and an AI study tutor (Ustaad), both grounded in Pakistani data, plus real labour-market trends and affordability analysis.
  4. Convince the people who actually control the decision — parents — with a bilingual, printable, data-backed report.

The v1 product is a fully functional web application: public landing page, Supabase authentication, a complete onboarding quiz with OCR marksheet scanning, eight authenticated feature pages, three server-side AI API routes, a production coming-soon gate, and a 28-case automated test suite. It is deployed on Vercel and versioned on GitHub.

Headline metric for v1. A student who completes the quiz learns their real NUST / medical aggregate instead of a score computed from a zero (see §15 — this was the platform's original data bug). This single fix changes the merit page from misleading to honest.

2. Problem & Opportunity

2.1 The post-FSc decision crisis

Pakistan's intermediate (FSc) cohort is among the largest in the world. The numbers that frame the decision are stark:

StatisticValueImplication
MDCAT candidates vs MBBS seats~180,000 candidates vs ~11,000 seatsRoughly 1 in 15 pre-medical students gets a seat — most need a real Plan B.
Youth (15–24) unemployment~12.6% (PBS LFS 2024–25)A wrong degree choice has an immediate, measurable cost.
IT exportsUS$4.6B (FY26), targeting US$15B by 2030Technology is the fastest-growing escape route for non-medical students.
NUST merit weight of entry test75% NETEntry-test strategy matters more than marksheet — almost nobody is told this.

2.2 The information gap

Students have access to plenty of information (merit lists, academy pamphlets, forums) but almost no counseling. The gap has four concrete causes, all of which v1 addresses directly:

  1. Aggregates are never computed honestly. Coaching academies quote inflated "closing merits". The real formulas (NUST 75/15/10, FAST 50/40/10, PMDC 50/40/10) are public but never applied to an individual's own marks. §15 documents the exact formulas.
  2. Money is never treated as a merit factor. Affordability, scholarships, and relocation ability change which institutions are realistic — but they're never collected, so never factored in.
  3. Parental pressure is ignored by every tool. The person making the final decision is usually the parent, and parents are persuaded by data, not by the student's enthusiasm. No existing tool produces a parent-facing argument.
  4. No one collects the student's own data. Before aftermediate, the platform's own pages read profile fields that nothing ever populated — the merit page computed aggregates from a default score of zero, and the "convince" page reset its inputs on every page load.
Market fit signal. The product is deliberately hyper-local: it speaks Urdu where it matters, uses Pakistani boards, cities, PKR budgets, and real university entry tests. Generic international career sites cannot provide this density of local relevance.

3. Product Vision & Goals

3.1 Vision

Every student in Pakistan who finishes intermediate school should be able to make their next decision with the same quality of data a paid career counselor would give them — for free, on their phone.

3.2 Strategic Goals (v1)

#GoalHow it shows up in the product
G1Collect the student's real position once, and reuse it everywhereSingle onboarding quiz feeds merit, money, convince, profile, career pages.
G2Make merit honestReal aggregate formulas applied to real marks; entry-test scores collected and used (never defaulted to zero).
G3Give students a voice with their parentsBilingual, printable parent report built from the student's real data.
G4Guide, don't lectureTwo AI assistants with distinct, hard-scoped roles (site guidance vs study tutoring).
G5Resume, don't restartQuiz progress survives tab-close and device switch (localStorage + Supabase).

3.3 Guiding Principles

  • Honesty over optimism. A hard truth ("your aggregate puts you in the top 25%") beats a flattering lie. Data is sourced and citable.
  • Local by default. Urdu where natural, PKR budgets, Pakistani boards and institutions, realistic scholarship info.
  • One source of truth. The quiz is the canonical home of student data; everything else reads it. Avoid duplicate state.
  • Data-driven UX. Questions and recommendations are data, not hardcoded JSX, so content changes are edits not surgery.

  • Questions are data (src/lib/quiz.ts): id is a dotted path (quiz.city, marks.fscObtained) so one setter writes anywhere without special-casing.
  • Conditional logic: entry-test scores hidden when entryTest = "none"; MDCAT only for pre-medical; ECAT only for pre-engineering; Part-1 shown only for NET.

6.3 Dashboard — /dashboard

  • Greeting ("Salam, [name]."), stream badge, FSc percentage badge.
  • Reality-check card for the user's stream.
  • Aggregate cards (NUST / FAST / MDCAT depending on stream) with links to the merit page.
  • "Fields that fit your stream" (up to 6 majors) with salary ranges and demand badges.
  • Shortcut cards to Merit / Career / Money / Convince.
  • "Talk to Rahbar" CTA (opens the slide-in drawer) and links to profile editing.

6.4 Profile — /profile

  • Hero: avatar upload (client-side, 2 MB image limit, data-URL stored), name and bio inline-editing, email, quiz-completion badge.
  • Stat strip: FSc %, skills count, interests count.
  • Editable sections: Academics (stream, board, year, entry test, marks), Education (add/remove rows: degree, institution, year, grade), Skills (40 toggleable chips), Interests (toggleable chips), Money & location, Decisions & pressure.
  • Every section uses a consistent Edit → inline form → Save/Cancel pattern.
  • "Edit quiz →" links back to /onboard.

6.5 Merit Engine — /merit

  • Computes and explains NUST, FAST (computing and engineering splits), and PMDC/MDCAT aggregates.
  • Interactive: any number can be adjusted and the aggregate recomputes live.
  • Shows the exact weight breakdown per university (see §15).
  • MDCAT percentile + "band" label (e.g. "Top 10%").
  • Data source links for every figure.

6.6 Career Explorer — /career and /career/[id]

  • List of majors filtered to the student's stream, with demand badges and salary ranges.
  • Detail page per major: description, why-now stat, skill tree, day-in-the-life simulator (scenario + choices + results), courses, universities.
  • Static data-backed; no AI required at view time.

6.7 Trends — /trends

  • Labour-market and industry data: jobs, industry exports, abroad destinations.
  • Charts (Recharts) and citable sources.

6.8 Money & Scholarships — /money

  • Reads the quiz's monthly budget (quiz.budgetMonthly) and computes affordability tiers at PKR 20k / 60k / 150k thresholds.
  • Scholarship directory (HEC, Fulbright, etc.) with eligibility and deadlines.

6.9 Convince (Parent Report) — /convince

  • A bilingual (English/Urdu) printable report for parents.
  • Seeded from the student's real profile data (replaces the old hardcoded 0,0,3,3 state).
  • Includes reality-check stats, a career-data chart, and a parent-facing takeaway in Urdu.
  • Print/PDF export via the browser.

6.10 Ustaad — Study Assistant — /study

  • Full-page AI tutor chat for FSc / MDCAT / NET / ECAT study help.
  • Persona-scoped prompt (see §13); explicitly not a general assistant.
  • Suggestion chips ("Make me a 2-week MDCAT revision plan", …), streaming responses, localStorage history.

6.11 Rahbar — Site Assistant (drawer)

  • Slide-in drawer from the right edge on any app page; opened from the sidebar "Talk to Rahbar" button or the dashboard CTA.
  • Persona-scoped to site guidance — how to use pages, what features do, where data lives. Redirects study questions to Ustaad.
  • Backdrop + close, streaming responses, localStorage history, suggestion chips.

10.2 Client-Side State & Sync

Student data lives in a single React context (src/lib/store.tsx) backed by localStorage. A global ProfileSync controller (mounted in the root layout) owns the two-way sync:

  1. Hydrate (remote → local): on sign-in, fetch the user's profiles row once. If the remote updated_at is newer than the local stamp, replace the store from remote; otherwise keep local.
  2. Push (local → remote): debounced ~800 ms upsert on every profile change (avoids the write storm of per-keystroke writes).
  3. Conflict rule: last-write-wins on updated_at. A single student on their own devices — acceptable.
  4. quizStep is intentionally not synced; cross-device resume uses the computed firstIncompleteSection.

10.3 Middleware / Routing Gate

src/proxy.ts runs on every request. In production, any path that is not / or /coming-soon is redirected to /coming-soon — this gates the public demo. In development, all pages are accessible so changes can be previewed. API routes are excluded from the matcher.

Operational caveat. Because the production gate sends the public demo to /coming-soon, the full app flow is only reachable in dev (or by relaxing the gate). A Vercel preview-deployment toggle is on the roadmap (§19).

11.5 Row-Level Security

All tables enable RLS. Policies restrict to the owner via auth.uid() = id (profiles) or auth.uid() = user_id (plans, chats). A handle_new_user trigger auto-creates the profile row on sign-up. Storage bucket marksheets is private with owner-scoped policies.

12. API Specification

12.1 POST /api/chat

Streams an AI response. Body: { persona, messages: {role, content}[] }. Personas: rahbar (site guide), study (Ustaad), essay, cv. Response is a text-stream (text/plain chunks). Runtime: Node, maxDuration=60.

12.2 POST /api/ocr

Parses a Pakistani FSc marksheet image (data URL) into structured marks JSON via the vision model. Strict output contract: { board, stream, matricObtained, matricTotal, fscObtained, fscTotal, fscPart1Obtained, fscPart1Total, subjects[], confidence }. Invalid/absent values are null, never guessed. Urdu digits are normalised to English.

12.3 POST /api/demand

Given { stream, interests, countries }, returns per-country labour-market insight lines via structured generation. Validates the stream against the allowed enum before calling the model.

Error convention. All API routes return 400 for invalid input and 500 for generation failures, with a JSON { error } body. Chat returns a streamed fallback string on failure rather than aborting.

13.1 Grounding & source discipline

  • Rahbar cites sources for stats (e.g. PBS LFS 2024–25 youth unemployment 12.6%).
  • Study help sticks to verifiable exam structure facts.
  • Structured generation (generateStructured) wraps model output in a JSON parse-and-extract step; OCR and demand routes use it.
  • Streaming (streamText) is used for chat so first tokens arrive quickly.

15. Aggregate Mathematics

These formulas are the product's backbone. They are implemented in src/lib/aggregates.ts, unit-tested, and used by the dashboard, merit page, and profile. pct = obtained / total × 100.

15.1 NUST

aggregate = NET% × 0.75 + FSc Part-I% × 0.15 + Matric% × 0.10

NET total defaults to 200. Part-I is used when present, else full FSc.

15.2 FAST

Computing: test% × 0.50 + HSSC% × 0.40 + SSC% × 0.10
Engineering: test% × 0.33 + HSSC% × 0.50 + SSC% × 0.17

Entry-test total defaults to 100 for FAST.

15.3 PMDC (Medical)

aggregate = MDCAT% × 0.50 + HSSC% × 0.40 + SSC% × 0.10
MDCAT total defaults to 200. Top public colleges close at ~91–96%.

15.4 Worth Score

A readiness score used on readiness/career surfaces: FSc% × .35 + certs×25 (cap 100) × .25 + projects×25 × .20 + english×20 × .10 + consistency×20 × .10, rounded to a band: Emerging · Building momentum · High potential.

15.5 Overall Standing

Pre-test ballpark: FSc% × 0.6 + Matric% × 0.4, mapped to percentile bands (top 1% … top 80%).

Why this section exists. v1's merit page originally computed every aggregate with an entry-test score of zero — NUST merit was wrong for every user. The onboarding quiz now collects the score, the formulas default totals only, and the tests pin the corrected math (§18).

16. Security & Privacy

  • Secrets: keys live in .env.local (git-ignored); .env.example documents names only. The Supabase service_role key is never used client-side. Never commit real keys.
  • RLS: every table has owner-scoped policies; new columns are covered by existing policies.
  • Input validation: API routes validate required fields and enums (e.g. stream) before hitting the model.
  • File uploads: client-side images are constrained (type image/*, ≤ 2 MB) and stored as data URLs; marksheet uploads go to a private, owner-scoped storage bucket.
  • Prompt injection hygiene: persona prompts are fixed server-side; user text is treated as message content, not system instructions.
  • Privacy: no analytics SDKs in v1; chat history is localStorage/client-side; no third-party tracking.
